      🔬 Our project · Part 1

      The problem

      The Palos Verdes blue butterfly is in trouble. Its caterpillars only eat a few plants, like deerweed. Houses, roads, and weeds are taking the place of those plants.

      It has been on the endangered list since 1980. That means there are very few left.

      Our question

      How might we help families on the peninsula grow the right plants so local pollinators have a connected home?

      🔬 Our project · Part 2

      What we learned

      • Wild plants that grow here feed the bugs that live here. Many caterpillars eat only 1 or 2 kinds of plants.
      • Small patches that are far apart are hard for animals to use. One long, connected strip works better.
      • Weeds from other places grow fast. They push out the plants that belong here.

      🌱 You can help

      Help at home

      1. Plant wild plants

      Pick plants that grow here naturally. See the list below.

      2. Pull weeds

      Pull them out early, before they spread.

      3. Team up

      Ask your neighbors to plant too. Gardens close together make a butterfly path.

      Plants in our kit

      • Deerweed: food for Palos Verdes blue caterpillars.
      • Santa Barbara milkvetch: the other plant Palos Verdes blue caterpillars eat.
      • California buckwheat: lots of tiny flowers with nectar for bees and butterflies.
      • California sagebrush: a soft grey bush. Small birds hide and nest in it.
      • Bush sunflower: bright yellow flowers that bees love.
      • Narrowleaf milkweed: food for monarch butterfly caterpillars.

      Ask a local native plant nursery which seeds are best for your yard. edit
